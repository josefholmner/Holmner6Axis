Info:

Motor: Nema 17 HS4401S
Motor Driver: drv8825
Pulse generator: Atmel Atmega 328 (Arduino Nano)
Motor Driver in-voltage: 20v
Motor Driver step mode: 1/4 step
Small gear tooth count: 10 
Large gear tooth count: 65
Gear ratio: 2:13
Wooden rod weight: 0.129kg
End weight (bottle): 0.418kg
Axis to wooden rod center-of-mass distance: 0.145m
Axis to weight (bottle) attachment distance: 0.30m

----------------------
Execution:

The motor rotated the arm about 100 degrees from a vertical position.
The speed was low (500 deg/s on the motor side) but still high enough for a realistic axis1 movement.
A ramp was used to allow the speed to build up, but max speed was reached long before the movement mid-point. 
The motor was programmed to stop after about 100 degrees.

----------------------
Result:

At most, the end weight (bottle) maximum weight achieved was 0.418kg.
The nearest failing experiment was at about 0.460kg, so the real maximum is anywhere in between those values.
Failing was due to motor skipping internally, NOT due to cog slipping. This was carefully ensured using markers on each cog.

Motor torque calculation (when the arm is at 90 degrees):
Reaction torque due to rod center-of-mass: 0.129 * 9.81 * 0.145 = 0.18349605 Nm
Reaction torque due to end weight (bottle): 0.418 * 9.81 * 0.30 = 1.230174 Nm
Sum of torques on arm-side (after gears): 0.18349605 + 1.230174 = 1.41367005 Nm
Motor torque: 1.41367005 * (2/13): 0.2174877 Nm

Result: Torque at the motor shaft during movement AT LEAST 0.2174877 Nm, likely a bit higher.

---------------------
Notes:

The Atmel Atmega 328 was used instead of a Raspberry Pi to ensure a clean stepping signal.

Running the motor in full-step mode (which should give higher torque according to many sources) caused the motor to give out lots of noise and the torque before slipping was greatly REDUCED.
Not sure why. A different motor was tested as well, with the same result. Might be due to a faulty Motor Driver. The pulse generating code was altered to compensate for the different step mode.
Running the motor in 1/8 step mode also made the motor run slower than expected.
I think the tests with the other stepping modes should be taken with a grain of salt since I did not get the motor to behave as expected.
